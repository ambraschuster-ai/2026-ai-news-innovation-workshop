Where Can You Take This?

Not many newsrooms are hiring an "AI journalist." What's actually happening is AI getting folded into the jobs that already exist, plus a small, real set of new AI roles cropping up.

01

Existing Jobs, New Capability

AI is showing up less as a new job title and more as a new capability folded into jobs that already exist. The clearest examples aren't engineering teams, they're individual reporters and data journalists doing the job they already had, with one more tool.

  • Andy Sullivan, a Reuters Washington correspondent with no programming background, has built 14 tools this way, including one that scans hundreds of daily Federal Register entries and flags what's newsworthy: a reporter augmenting their own investigation, not a new role.*
  • Joe Amditis built and now maintains a public library of 50+ Claude Code Skills for journalism tasks, FOIA requests, verification, scraping, data pipelines, entirely on his own: a data journalist extending what the job already covers.*
  • J.D. Capelouto, a Semafor breaking-news reporter, started experimenting with ChatGPT's GPT Builder and ended up building the tool that now powers Semafor's Signals feed.*

All three started the same way: a repetitive task they disliked, no budget, and nobody's permission needed. That pattern, not a job posting, is the reality for many people in the industry right now.

It's also part of a broader shift. Newsroom public code repositories fell from over 2,000 a year in 2016 to under 400 by 2024, an 80% collapse, as the old model of dedicated engineering teams building tools took over.* That's reversing: by early 2026, roughly 37% of new newsroom code repositories showed signs of AI-assisted building, growth that accelerated sharply starting around September 2025.* Most of that building is people like Sullivan, Amditis, and Capelouto, not people hired into a newly invented role.

02

AI Roles That Do Exist

A smaller, real set of dedicated titles are cropping up, distinct from the augmentation happening everywhere else:

Newsroom Architect, Politico
Bridges editorial and technical strategy, builds AI tools for investigative search, coordinates the newsroom's architecture roadmap.*
Computational Journalist, ProPublica
Built custom OCR software processing 7,000+ government files and a tool transcribing 2,000+ hours of radio into searchable timelines, before joining ProPublica's data team.*
Senior Editor, AI Innovation, CNN
Prototypes, tests, and deploys AI-powered tools and workflows directly with reporters.*
Newsroom AI Lead, Business Insider
Audits which AI tools reporters and editors are already using, then sets editorial AI strategy and keeps it inside newsroom standards.*
VP, AI Labs, NPR
Business-side lead for NPR's AI Labs, co-wrote NPR's AI guidelines and ran its pilot programs before joining full time.*
Editorial Director of AI Initiatives, The New York Times
Leads the Times' dedicated AI team, set the paper's editorial principles for generative AI, runs staff training on applying it to reporting.*
Global Editor, AI Development & Integration, Reuters
Leads AI-driven asset association work that's cut a multi-minute manual process down to under a minute.*

A recent industry survey of senior editors across 50+ countries ranked "coding and product development" the third most important AI use case for publishers, with 81% calling it important.*

03

Before You Present

You started Day 1 with a problem statement on a whiteboard. Before you show what you built, take a few minutes with what actually changed.

  • What's one thing you understand now that you didn't three days ago?
  • What's one habit from this week that you can already tell you'll keep using?
  • What is one way you plan to use these skills moving forward?

🎯 Apply This

Time: A few minutes, individually, then share with a neighbor.

Description: Pick one answer above. Work it into your show and tell, not as an add-on at the end, but as part of the story of what you built and how you got there.

Deliverable: One sentence you'll actually say out loud during your presentation.
