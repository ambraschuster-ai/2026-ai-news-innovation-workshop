Human-AI Design

Neither human-in-the-loop nor full automation is the safe default. Both are design decisions.

11:35 AM·35 MIN·Lecture
01

Human in the Loop Isn't the Default

Sometimes you add a human step you don't strictly need, because they benefit from sitting with the output. Other times their perspective isn't needed at all, so you automate top to bottom and check only the final result.

  • Would a human actually make this better, or just slower? Pairing tends to hurt on judgment calls, help on drafting.
  • Is the human a real decision-maker, or a rubber stamp? A checkpoint only counts if they have context, authority, and time to override.
  • Will the reviewer stay sharp enough to catch mistakes? Skills that only get monitored, not used, decay.
  • Is this a domain where oversight isn't optional, legally or otherwise?
02

Human-in-the-loop in Practice

Full Fact draws an explicit line: automate the filtering, never the judgment call.

Fully automated
Classification, extraction, ranking, matching against past fact-checks.
Never automated
Deciding if a claim is true, writing the fact-check itself.

AP's rule: any AI output is "unvetted source material," staff still apply normal sourcing standards before it goes near publication.

03

Humans can make things better or worse

Zillow removed a working checkpoint under growth pressure, purchase volume doubled, then a $421M quarterly loss followed and the business shut down.

This doesn't mean automation beats humans. Whether you keep the human or remove them is a specific, testable design choice for each task, not a value applied uniformly.

04

Same Judgment, When You're Coding

Full Fact's filtering and Zillow's checkpoint are the same decision you make constantly while you build: does this step need a human?

A formatting fix is cheap to undo, let it commit. A change to how your app handles credentials is expensive to undo, read it line by line. Plan Mode is this same choice, built in: a checkpoint you add on purpose before something costly, so you catch a wrong assumption while it's still cheap to fix.
