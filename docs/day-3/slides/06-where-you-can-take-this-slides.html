Where Can You Take This?

Not many newsrooms are hiring an "AI journalist." AI is getting folded into the jobs that already exist, plus a small, real set of new AI roles cropping up.

3:15 PM·30 MIN·Lecture
01

Existing Jobs, New Capability

AI is showing up less as a new job title and more as a capability folded into jobs that already exist.

  • A Reuters correspondent with no coding background has built 14 tools, augmenting his own investigations.
  • One journalist solo-built and maintains 50+ Claude Code Skills for journalism, extending what the job already covers.
  • A Semafor reporter started tinkering with ChatGPT's GPT Builder and ended up building the tool that now powers Semafor's Signals feed.

All three started the same way: a repetitive task they disliked, no budget, nobody's permission needed.

02

AI Roles That Do Exist

A smaller, real set of dedicated titles are cropping up, distinct from the augmentation happening everywhere else.

Newsroom Architect, Politico
Bridges editorial and technical strategy.
Computational Journalist, ProPublica
Built OCR and transcription tools before joining.
Senior Editor, AI Innovation, CNN
Prototypes and deploys tools with reporters.
Newsroom AI Lead, Business Insider
Audits AI tool use, sets editorial AI strategy.
VP, AI Labs, NPR
Co-wrote NPR's AI guidelines, ran its pilots.
Editorial Director of AI Initiatives, NYT
Set the Times' generative-AI principles.
Global Editor, AI Development & Integration, Reuters
Leads AI-driven asset association work.

"Coding and product development" ranks 3rd most important AI use case for publishers, 81% importance, in a 50+ country survey.

Before You Present

  • What's one thing you understand now that you didn't three days ago?
  • What's one habit from this week you can already tell you'll keep using?
  • What is one way you plan to use these skills moving forward?
