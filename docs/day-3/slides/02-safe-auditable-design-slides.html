Safe & Auditable Design

"Responsible AI" sounds like a value statement. What it actually means is how our promises map to our behaviors.

10:55 AM·40 MIN·Lecture
01

What "Auditable" Means

Which actions would we need to explain later, if someone asked why the tool did what it did?

For most projects, that's four inquiries: what went in, what came out, what the system did with it, and whether it was allowed.

02

When It Goes Wrong

NYC's MyCity chatbot
Gave false legal guidance. Kept giving the same wrong answers for over a year after being exposed, no monitoring loop caught it.
Air Canada's chatbot
Gave a wrong discount policy. Airline argued the bot was "a separate legal entity." A tribunal rejected that and made them pay.

The two safety axes here are responsiveness and accountability, central tenets of responsible AI.

03

Behavior is the key

90% of 52 studied newsroom policies required disclosure. Only 8% specified how it would be enforced.

  • WBEZ: approval per instance, quarterly Oversight Committee.
  • LAist: "a human has reviewed it and that person is accountable."
  • CBC: one test question, would the audience be misled without disclosure?
04

A Working Checklist

Simplified from NIST's AI Risk Management Framework.

  • What is AI being used for, and where shouldn't it be used?
  • What happens when it fails, not just if it's right?
  • Do you have a kill switch?
  • Can a user flag a wrong answer, and does anyone look?

05

Design for Transparency

Telling people you used AI is an interface decision, and the details of that decision measurably change trust.

Detailed disclosures actually made readers slightly less trusting than a one-line label, even though most preferred the detailed version. Researchers call this the "transparency dilemma."

One study made this concrete: 93% of an AI article's claims linked back to their source, versus 25% for human-written comparisons, and readers rated it more transparent. Six patterns for giving readers that choice:

  • Highlight-for-glancing: mark the passages AI touched, scannable without breaking flow.
  • Info button: a small icon readers click for full disclosure, not a forced block of text.
  • Outlet-level disclosure: publish your AI policy once, not per article.
  • Proportional AI-ratio visualization: a percentage bar showing how much AI touched.
  • Visual trust stamp: a badge for responsible AI use.
  • "No AI used" label: disclosure as a signal that works both directions.
